The rundown

Four beats, about five seconds, in any app with a text cursor: Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, Slack, Mail, your terminal.

  1. 0:00 ON AIR

    Tap the key

    Caps Lock (or fn) starts recording. A small pill near the bottom of your screen says it is listening.

  2. 0:01

    Talk like you think

    Ums, restarts, half sentences. Hold the key instead for push-to-talk. Escape throws the take away.

  3. 0:04

    Tap again, or Return

    Return stops recording and is not sent to your app, so a half-written message never goes out by accident.

  4. 0:05

    Clean text lands

    Whisper transcribes, a local model tidies it, and it is pasted where your cursor is. Lists become lists; file names get backticks.

Nothing you say leaves the room.

Many dictation apps send your voice to a server. Out of the box, LocalFlow never does: both models run on your Mac's own chip. No account, no analytics. The audio file is deleted after each dictation.

  • SpeechWhisper small.en, through whisper.cpp, on your Mac.
  • CleanupQwen 2.5 7B (Apache 2.0), through llama.cpp, on your Mac.
  • NetworkUsed once, to download the two models from Hugging Face. Each file is checked against its published checksum.
  • AccountNone. There is nothing to sign in to.
  • TelemetryNone. The app never reports usage, so we count downloads, not people.

It cleans. It never answers.

Talk to an agent and you'll ask questions and give orders. A dictation tool that answers them, or writes the code itself, has ruined your prompt. LocalFlow hands them back cleaned up, ready to send.
